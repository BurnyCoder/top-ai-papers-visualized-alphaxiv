# Mapping scientific papers by topic

The scientific question is whether a two-dimensional view of highly liked papers preserves their semantic neighborhoods. alphaXiv supplies the selection signal. An embedding supplies a separate definition of relatedness. A direct precedent is Murray et al., who embedded scientific titles and abstracts, clustered documents, and compared descriptive language-model labels with labels made from characteristic terms. Their results motivate showing representative papers with each generated name. They do not establish how any particular grouping will perform on this collection. [Murray et al., full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02601v1)

McInnes, Healy and Melville construct a weighted neighborhood graph and optimize its low-dimensional representation. Local structure can change during projection. The reference implementation's clustering guide specifically describes artificial separations and density distortion. We will therefore calculate paper recommendations from the original vectors, fit clusters in a separate ten-dimensional space, and measure how many neighbors survive in the screen coordinates. [UMAP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.03426) · [Clustering guide](https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/clustering.html)

HDBSCAN selects stable branches of a density hierarchy and can leave points unassigned. Its minimum cluster size and minimum samples control different aspects of the result. Reporting agreement only on confidently assigned items can exaggerate apparent performance, so the atlas will also report the assigned fraction. [Algorithm](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.00205) · [Parameter guide](https://hdbscan.readthedocs.io/en/latest/parameter_selection.html)

Huang, Wang, Rudin and Browne benchmarked neighborhood preservation to quantify projection distortion on single-cell data. Their application differs from scientific text. The overlap statistic transfers directly because it only requires two neighbor rankings for the same items. Our comparison is conditional on a frozen set of papers and one hosted representation. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-022-03628-x) · [ML research pitfalls](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.02497) · [NeurIPS reporting guidance](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist)

## Data provenance

The production REST feed supports a global likes order, pagination, a time interval and blog filters. Its returned public vote field matches the number displayed on the paper page in the initial check. A FreshRSS integration reads the same field but caps requests in its own code. We use the service directly and verify the boundary with two complete passes. This is a census of eligible records exposed by the public feed during a documented window. [API specification](https://api-dev.alphaxiv.org/api.json) · [FreshRSS implementation](https://github.com/shcheglovnd/FreshRSS-xExtension-AlphaXivPapers/blob/main/lib/AlphaXivPapers.php) · [Checked paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2504.13181)
